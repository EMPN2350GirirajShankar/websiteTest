## The challenge

A modern Formula 1 season is a logistics problem as much as an engineering one. Roughly 2,000 people support a calendar of 24 races spread across 21 countries, and the operation splits between a permanent factory base and a travelling trackside crew that rebuilds itself every other week.

That creates three constant pressures:

- **Knowledge is scattered.** Setup notes, maintenance history, and supplier documentation live across SharePoint, Outlook, and line-of-business systems. Finding the right answer under time pressure is its own task.
- **Routine work crowds out engineering.** Approvals, notifications, part checks, and status chasing consume hours that should go into making the car faster.
- **Systems have to be available, always.** A race weekend has no maintenance window. <mark>If a system is down during a session, the decision it supports simply does not get made.</mark>

## The approach

Rather than building bespoke tooling for every problem, the team standardized on the Microsoft platform it already ran on and layered AI and low-code automation on top.

### Put knowledge one question away

Microsoft 365 Copilot sits across the apps people already use, handling knowledge discovery, meeting summaries, and task follow-up. Copilot Studio extends that with specialized agents wired into the systems where operational data actually lives — SharePoint, Outlook, and ServiceNow — so a question about a part, a process, or a past incident gets answered without a hunt through folders.

### Digitize the workflows around the car

Power Apps replaced paper-and-spreadsheet processes with low-code applications that standardize maintenance and operational workflows for frontline and engineering teams. Power Automate handles the connective tissue: approvals, notifications, and the routine handoffs that used to depend on someone remembering to send an email.

## What to take from it

The pattern here is not F1-specific. Any organization running a distributed operation against a fixed calendar faces the same three questions:

1. **Where does knowledge live, and how long does it take to retrieve?** If the answer is measured in minutes of searching, an agent layered over existing systems pays for itself quickly.
2. **Which routine work is stealing engineering time?** Approvals and notifications are usually the cheapest automations with the largest reclaimed hours.
3. **What breaks if a system is unavailable at the worst moment?** Availability is a business requirement, not an IT metric.
